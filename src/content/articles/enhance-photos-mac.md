# How to Auto-Enhance Photos on Mac (Color, Contrast, Red-Eye)

Some photos just need a nudge — the color's a little flat, the contrast is off, someone's eyes went red under the flash. You don't want to open a full editor and start pushing sliders for a quick fix. You want the obvious problems gone and the file saved.

Converleon's improved **Enhance** does that in one pass. It corrects color, contrast, and red-eye automatically, then saves the result in a format that suits the photo. It's offline, so the images stay on your Mac.

## One-click correction, no editor required

Enhance isn't a darkroom. It handles the routine fixes most snapshots need and leaves you with a cleaner file, without a panel of controls to learn.

## What you need

- A Mac on macOS 13.5 or later
- Converleon from the App Store
- The photos you want to clean up

## Step by step

**1. Drop your photos.** Drag one image or a batch onto the Converleon icon.

**2. Choose Enhance.** Pick the Enhance action. Converleon adjusts color and contrast and clears red-eye automatically.

**3. Done.** The corrected photo saves to your Mac in an optimized format — JPG, PNG, or HEIC, chosen from the source file and whether it has transparency.

## How it picks the output format

You don't set the format yourself. Enhance reads the source and saves where it makes sense: PNG when the image carries transparency, HEIC for HEIC-family files, JPG for ordinary photos. The point is a clean result without a format decision on top of it.

## Where it fits

Enhance pairs naturally with the other image tools. Clean up a photo, then [remove its background](/blog/remove-background-mac) for a product shot, or [compress it](/blog/compress-files-mac) for the web. And because it runs locally, family photos and client images never leave your drive — the [offline approach](/blog/stop-using-online-converters) the whole app is built on.

## Two things people get wrong

- **Expecting heavy retouching.** Enhance is automatic correction, not a manual editor — it fixes the common stuff, it won't reshape a composition.
- **Running it on an already-edited file.** Auto-correction works best on the original. Stacking it on top of edits you've already made can fight your earlier choices.
